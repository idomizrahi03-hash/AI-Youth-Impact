# AI Risk Radar Framework

This document outlines the analytical framework for identifying behavioral and usage patterns that may indicate cognitive over-reliance or unbalanced AI interaction among adolescents.

---

## Methodological Boundaries & Ethics

> **Mandatory Disclaimer:** 
> The AI Risk Radar is **not** a psychological, clinical, or medical diagnostic tool. It is an experimental product framework designed to identify behavioral usage indicators and trends within data.

---

## Risk Indicators Taxonomy

### 1. Cognitive Offloading
- **Definition:** Excessive transfer of core thinking, memory, and analytical tasks to AI without independent cognitive processing.
- **Data Indicators:** High frequency of Level 3/4 usage, high copy-paste ratio, low output verification.
- **Theoretical Basis:** Cognitive Load Theory & Technology-Induced Offloading literature.

### 2. Decision Dependency
- **Definition:** Reduction in autonomous decision-making and uncritical acceptance of AI suggestions.
- **Data Indicators:** High proportion of action-seeking prompts, lack of alternative exploration.
- **Theoretical Basis:** Automation Bias literature in Human Factors research.

### 3. Emotional Reliance
- **Definition:** Primary utilization of conversational AI for emotional regulation, stress relief, and personal validation.
- **Data Indicators:** High volume of emotional support category logs, late-night usage patterns.
- **Theoretical Basis:** Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) & Parasocial Interaction research.

### 4. Social Substitution
- **Definition:** Preferring AI interaction over human peer, family, or educational engagement.
- **Data Indicators:** Skewed ratio between AI screen time and collaborative group work.
- **Theoretical Basis:** Media Studies & Adolescent Social Development literature.

### 5. Excessive / Unbalanced Use
- **Definition:** High-volume daily usage that displaces other necessary developmental or academic activities.
- **Data Indicators:** Daily total active hours exceeding upper-percentile thresholds, session frequency.
- **Theoretical Basis:** Digital Wellbeing & Screen-time research guidelines.
